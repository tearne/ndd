# Surface-then-one-at-a-time as a core principle

## Intent

Elevate **"surface the whole set, then engage one item at a time"** from an incidental pattern to a first-class NDD principle, and weave it through the method wherever the agent presents batched material. The pattern already appears locally — the [Approach](#approach)'s Unresolved walkthrough, and [Interaction Grain](#interaction-grain)'s one-node-at-a-time rule for map edits — but it is nowhere named as the single underlying discipline, so it is easy to honour in one place and breach in another (e.g. proposing several map nodes at once).

The principle protects a specific failure mode: **attention dies under overwhelm**. Giving the user the big picture (the list) preserves orientation; drip-feeding the detail (one at a time) preserves engagement. Dumping everything at once destroys both — the user can neither hold it all nor engage deeply with any of it. This can be framed as a *fourth death* alongside the three in **Non-Dead Design (a3k)** (specifications, structural thinking, comprehension): the death of attention.

Scope when picked up: name/frame the principle (candidate: a new child under **Principles (p4c)**, or fold into the root's deaths framing), then ensure it is referenced everywhere batched material is surfaced — Approach Unresolved, Interaction Grain, multi-node map edits, and any future batch surfaces. Relates to Enjoyment (artifact economy) and Interaction Grain.
